## First, the two-minute recovery playbook

The stream is down, the service is still going, and someone just texted "is the stream supposed to be off?" Work this list in order — each step takes seconds:

1. **Check the encoder first.** Is OBS/vMix still running and still "streaming"? A crashed encoder is the most common cause and the fastest fix — restart the stream output, not the whole machine.
2. **Check the encoder's connection status.** Dropped frames climbing? Bitrate at zero? That's network, not software. Move to step 3. Bitrate healthy but platform shows offline? That's downstream — step 4.
3. **Check the network path.** Can the booth machine load any website? If not: check the switch/router the encoder hangs off. If the building's internet is down entirely, your recovery is a phone hotspot on the encoder — lower the bitrate to 2,500 Kbps first.
4. **Check the platform.** If the encoder says it's sending and the destination says nothing is arriving, restart the stream output to force a fresh connection. If one destination is down but others are live, it's that platform's problem — let it go and carry on.
5. **Post once, calmly.** One message on the platform where your congregation watches: "We had a technical issue — the stream is back, and the full service will be posted after." Then stop managing comments and finish the service.

What you should **not** do mid-service: reboot the booth machine on a hunch, re-type stream keys, or change multiple settings at once. Every uncontrolled change makes the post-service diagnosis harder.

## After the service: find the real cause

A dropped stream always has a story, and the story is in the data — if anything was recording it. The questions that matter:

- What did the **bitrate history** look like in the ten minutes before the drop? A slow decline points to network congestion; a cliff points to a crash or disconnect.
- Was the **encoder under load**? CPU spikes from a scene change, an update running in the background, a thermal throttle?
- Did anything else on the network change — a backup kicking off, a room full of phones hitting guest WiFi?
- Has this happened before, on a pattern? Third Sunday, cold mornings, the week someone "tidied" the rack?

If your answer to these is "we have no idea," that's not a diagnosis problem — it's a monitoring problem.

## The real fix: find out before the congregation does

Churches that recover from stream drops in seconds instead of scrambling all have the same architecture:

- **Continuous stream-health monitoring** — bitrate, dropped frames, and encoder load watched in real time, with history you can look back on
- **Alerts that fire on degradation, not just failure** — you want the "bitrate is sagging" warning while there's still time to act
- **One feed out via restreaming** — so a single recovery action restores every destination at once (see [restreaming for churches](/kb/articles/restreaming-for-churches-one-feed/))
- **Automatic recording upstream** — so even a rough stream night still produces a clean archive to post
- **A human to call** — because the worst version of this story is being alone with it at 9:07am
