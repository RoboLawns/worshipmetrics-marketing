## The short answer: hire builders for building — and stop asking builders to run Sundays

"Should we hire a church AV company?" is really two questions wearing one search box, and untangling them saves churches real money and real frustration.

**Question one: who should design and install our system?** For anything physical — a new sanctuary system, a renovation, rigging, acoustic treatment, a major camera or console upgrade — the answer is often *yes, hire professionals.* A good integrator has seen a hundred rooms like yours, knows why the subwoofer can't go there, and will save you from the five-figure mistakes DIY installs make. This is their craft; honor it.

**Question two — the one most searches are actually about: who helps us *run* this thing every week?** Different question, different answer, and hiring an install company to solve it is like calling your home builder every time a light switch confuses a houseguest. Here's how to get both layers right.

## Hiring the builder well

If you're in genuine build territory, a few field-tested rules: get **multiple bids** and ask specifically for **church references** — worship rooms have constraints (volunteer operators, silence-sensitive services, Sunday deadlines) that corporate AV firms sometimes underestimate. Favor the bid that asks about your *volunteers* over the one that only talks gear. And put one non-negotiable in the contract: **documentation of everything installed** — every device, connection, and cable run, labeled. Better still, have it land in a living [Signal Map](/system-documentation/) instead of a PDF that's stale by Christmas; the install's value compounds for a decade when [every future helper starts from facts](/kb/articles/remote-church-tech-support/). If your build is really an incremental upgrade, [the roadmap guide](/kb/budget/church-av-upgrade-roadmap/) may save you a bid process entirely.

## Why the weekly layer can't be hired by the visit

Integrators are project businesses: quote, install, punch list, next job. The weekly realities of a church booth — [the volunteer question at 8:45](/kb/articles/sunday-morning-church-tech-support/), [the settings that drifted](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/), [the stream that stuttered at 9:40](/kb/articles/livestream-stutters-40-minutes-in/), the new student who [needs training](/kb/articles/train-obs-volunteers-church/) — arrive fifty-two times a year on a schedule no service call fits. Churches that try to buy this layer by the visit end up with invoices that dwarf the install, or (more often) quietly stop calling and [drop everything back on one volunteer](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/).

The operation layer isn't a vendor. It's a *system* that lives with your booth: [monitoring that watches around the clock](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/), [automation that fixes routine failures](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) before anyone calls anybody, [AI support that knows your exact wiring](/luminos-ai-assistant/) answering at any hour, human pros — Care on weekdays, Sunday Assurance on Sundays — [who see your system the moment you dial](/kb/articles/church-av-tech-support-options/), and a [trained team](/kb/articles/get-obs-volunteers-church/) as the deepest layer of all. That stack starts [genuinely free](/kb/articles/small-church-tech-support-budget/) and scales by choice — which no truck roll ever did.

## The middle case: expertise by the engagement, not the install

Between "build my room" and "run my Sundays" sits a third need: *make us better at what we own.* Encoder settings audits, camera placement and switching workflow, director training, [a YouTube channel that actually gets discovered](/professional-services/) — targeted engagements, virtual or on-site, from people who live in church production. WorshipMetrics' own [professional services](/professional-services/) sit here deliberately: not competing with your integrator's install, but tuning the humans and workflows that run what they built.

## The one-line decision framework

**Physical and design → hire an integrator, insist on documentation. Weekly operation → build the living support stack. Skills and workflow → book targeted expertise.** Churches get into trouble only when they push one of these three at a job belonging to another — and the most common version, by far, is expecting the install company to be the operations partner it was never built to be. Sort the question you're actually asking, and every layer gets cheaper, kinder, and more likely to answer when Sunday needs it.
