## 1:00 p.m. — the blank order of service

*A composite hour, assembled from the Thursday routines of worship leaders who got their evenings back.*

Jordan leads worship at a two-service church, and Thursday, 1:00 to 2:00, is the planning hour — all of it, for the coming Sunday and a light pass over the month. It used to sprawl across four evenings and three apps. Here's where the hour goes now.

## 1:05 — songs and keys

The setlist comes together in the [song library](/songs/): two familiar congregation songs, the new one they've been teaching for three weeks, and a hymn for communion. Each song in the library carries its arrangement, default key, and — the part Jordan never thinks about until renewal season — its CCLI number, authors, and copyright line as structured fields.

Keys take thirty seconds instead of a rechart. The charts are stored [as ChordPro](/kb/articles/chordpro-worship-team-reference/) — one chart, every key — so setting Sunday's key to D means the vocalist sees D, the guitarist capos from C, and nobody maintains a second document. When Jordan fixed a wrong chord in the bridge last month, it fixed itself in every key at once.

(The library came over from their old presentation software [in one import](/kb/articles/migrate-propresenter-song-library/), CCLI metadata riding along — the one-time job that makes every Thursday after it shorter.)

## 1:20 — the plan, and both service times

The order of service goes onto the plan: songs, scripture, transitions, the baptism at the 11:00 only. The 9:00 and 11:00 are distinct service times on one plan, which matters twice — once now, for [per-time volunteer coverage](/kb/articles/scheduling-volunteers-month-at-a-time/), and once on Sunday, when each service's live run keeps its own record.

Jordan's drummer can only make the 9:00 this week. The plan says so honestly: 9:00 covered, 11:00 an open cell that still needs a name — not a date column that lies green while the second service quietly has no drummer.

## 1:25 — staffing the month, silently

Twenty minutes in the [scheduling matrix](/kb/articles/volunteer-scheduling-without-text-scramble/): vocals, band, and production positions down the left, the next four Sundays across the top. Jordan pencils in the rotation — the matrix flags one blockout (bass player, fall break) and one genuine double-booking, warns, and blocks nothing, because the software assumes the leader knows things it doesn't.

Nothing has emailed anyone. That's the feature Jordan would defend with their life: **drafting is thinking, and thinking is private.** At 1:45 the month looks right, and one Send batches every invite — each volunteer receiving their whole month at once, with one-tap accept and decline links that need no login. Declines will reopen cells and notify the team leader automatically; day-before reminders will send themselves. The scramble is structurally over.

## 1:50 — Sunday's charts reach the stage

Last five minutes: confirming what the musicians see. Sunday morning, the [stage display](/stage-display-software/) will carry each player's chart in their key, advancing with the service — and the rehearsal runthrough on Saturday will be a *draft* run, so it [never pollutes the service history](/kb/articles/live-run-test-vs-real-service/). Only Sunday's committed runs become the permanent record of what was actually sung.

## The part nobody sees

That committed-run detail is quietly doing Jordan's least favorite job. Every song performed in a real service accumulates as evidence; the spontaneous chorus the band flowed into two weeks ago took thirty seconds to log afterward and now lives in a ledger no plan-cleanup can erase. When the CCLI renewal email arrives next spring, the [trailing-year export](/kb/articles/ccli-reporting-with-lyrics-software/) — date, title, authors, CCLI number, evidence level — is a download, not a reconstruction.

Jordan closes the laptop at 2:01. Setlist planned, keys set, charts live, a month staffed, and a copyright report filing itself as a byproduct of Sunday happening. The planning hour is an hour again — which was the whole point: the [service planning](/services/) exists so Thursday afternoons can go back to writing the things the plans are *for*.
