## Two very different things share one name

"Cloud video production" is having a moment, and for churches it's worth slowing down on, because the phrase covers two genuinely different architectures — and the one most vendors sell is the one churches want least.

**Model one: cloud-hosted production.** The switching and compositing run on rented servers somewhere. You send inputs up, the cloud builds the program, it comes back down. It's powerful, and it makes sense for a distributed broadcast team with no shared facility. For a church with a booth and a Sunday morning, it means an ongoing compute bill and a live service that now depends on a solid, low-latency internet round-trip *for the switch itself* — the one thing you can least afford to gamble on at 10:00 a.m.

**Model two: local production, cloud-connected.** The switch stays in your booth, on the [vMix](/kb/articles/cloud-vmix-production-church/) or [OBS](/kb/articles/cloud-obs-production-church/) machine you already own — free, instant, reliable, working even if the internet hiccups. The *cloud* adds what a church actually lacks: the ability to see, reach, and support that booth from anywhere. This is the model [WorshipMetrics](/cloud-control/) is built on, and the rest of this post is why it's the right one for a church that wants to run its own production.

## Why "local switch, cloud connect" fits churches

Three church realities make this the better architecture, not just the cheaper one:

- **Reliability first.** Your Sunday switch cannot depend on your upload staying perfect for 75 minutes. Local switching means a network sag [degrades the stream gracefully](/kb/articles/church-wifi-good-enough-to-livestream/) — it never freezes the service in the room. The cloud layer is additive; if it blips, the service carries on.
- **You already bought the gear.** The booth PC, the switcher, the cameras — paid for. Cloud-hosted production asks you to rent compute you already own the equivalent of. Local-first means [local control is free, forever, on every plan](/pricing/); the cloud layer is the only thing you add.
- **Churches want to do it themselves.** This is the heart of it. WorshipMetrics isn't a production company you hand your feed to — it's the platform for the church that wants its *own* volunteers running its *own* booth, with the cloud making that team possible instead of replacing it.

## "Cloud production" undersells what's actually here

Here's the part that reframes the whole category: for a church, remote *switching* is the least interesting thing the cloud adds. The connection that carries control also carries four things a generic production service doesn't:

1. **Remote monitoring.** [Live telemetry from the booth](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/) — machine health, stream and recording state, encoder performance — so the tech director sees the service from a phone, and [an alert fires](/kb/articles/church-stream-drop-phone-alert/) the moment something drops.
2. **Collaboration.** A veteran or [a mentor guiding a student](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/) sees preview and program live and can assist — or take the wheel — from anywhere. This is what turns one operator into [a team](/kb/articles/you-already-have-vmix/).
3. **Text and team communication.** [Team chat](/team-chat/) where the service plan and the booth already live — not a separate app and a group text.
4. **Support that can see your system.** [910-WORSHIP](/phone-support/) and the in-app assistant start from your actual wiring and telemetry, not "describe your setup."

That's why calling it "cloud production" undersells it. It's remote production, monitoring, collaboration, and support riding on one secure outbound connection — no port forwarding, no VPN, only people you've invited, every action logged.

## The consolidation underneath

And there's a second reason a church specifically benefits: the same platform that adds the cloud layer also [collapses the stack of tools you're paying for separately](/kb/articles/one-platform-church-tech-stack/) — streaming, clipping, presentation, scheduling, chat, monitoring, documentation — into one login and one bill. Cloud production for a church isn't a point solution bolted onto five other subscriptions. It's one of the things the *platform* does, alongside everything else the booth and the church office need.
