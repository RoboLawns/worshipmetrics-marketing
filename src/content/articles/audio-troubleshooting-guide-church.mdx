## How to use this guide when the service starts in twenty minutes

This is a field guide, not a lecture. Scan the ten symptoms below, find the one in your headphones, and run its fast checks **in order** — each links to a full deep dive if the quick version doesn't land the fix. Audio's master rule: **follow the meters upstream**. Somewhere between the microphone and the platform there's a meter that moves and, right after it, one that doesn't — the break lives between them. And as everywhere in the booth: change one thing at a time, and say what you touched.

This guide is part of a series — the booth's other front doors are [OBS](/kb/articles/obs-troubleshooting-guide-church/), [vMix](/kb/articles/vmix-troubleshooting-guide-church/), [ProPresenter](/kb/articles/propresenter-troubleshooting-guide-church/), and [PTZ camera](/kb/articles/ptz-camera-troubleshooting-guide-church/) troubleshooting, and the pre-service half of all of them lives on one [printable checklist](/sunday-checklist/).

## 1. The stream is silent — but the room sounds fine

The room proves the mixer works; the silence proves the *stream feed* doesn't. Fast sequence: is the encoder's audio meter moving? If yes, the problem is downstream (track selection, a muted output, the platform); if no, walk upstream — the mixer's stream bus level, the USB/interface routing, the cable. Nine times out of ten it's a mute or a bus assignment, not hardware.

Deep dives: [no audio on YouTube or Facebook Live](/kb/troubleshooting/no-audio-youtube-facebook-stream/), and [mixer USB audio not recognized](/kb/troubleshooting/mixer-usb-not-recognized-computer/).

## 2. The USB mixer feed vanished after a reboot or update

Windows re-enumerated the devices overnight and the encoder is now listening to a "USB Audio Device" that no longer exists — or the console's USB mode changed. Fast sequence: re-select the device in the encoder, confirm the console's USB routing still sends the buses you think it does, and check the sample rate matches on both ends.

Deep dives: [mixer USB not recognized by the streaming PC](/kb/troubleshooting/mixer-usb-not-recognized-computer/), and the [X32-specific USB fixes](/kb/troubleshooting/behringer-x32-usb-audio-not-working/).

## 3. The stream is too quiet — or slamming into the red

Platforms normalize loudness, listeners ride tiny phone speakers, and the level that looks right on the board can land wrong online. Fast sequence: check the encoder meter's average level (peaks around −6 dB, speech sitting healthy, never pinned), fix it at the *stream bus*, not the master, and listen to the actual platform output for thirty seconds — not just the local meters.

Deep dive: [stream audio too loud or too quiet, calibrated properly](/kb/troubleshooting/streaming-mix-too-loud-quiet/).

## 4. Crackling, popping, or dropouts

Digital audio's signature complaint, and almost always a *clocking or buffer* problem: two devices disagreeing about the sample rate, a buffer set optimistically small, or a USB cable/port with an opinion. Fast sequence: match sample rates end to end (48 kHz everywhere is the church standard), raise the buffer, and move the USB cable off the hub onto a direct port.

Deep dive: [crackling and popping on the stream, root-caused](/kb/troubleshooting/crackle-popping-audio-church-stream/).

## 5. Echo or feedback on the stream

Echo online means the same signal is arriving twice a few hundred milliseconds apart — classically a microphone hearing the room *plus* a direct feed, or monitoring looped back into the mix. Feedback that only the stream hears follows the same logic: find the second path and close it.

Deep dive: [echo and feedback on the church live stream](/kb/troubleshooting/echo-feedback-church-live-stream/).

## 6. Audio drifts out of sync as the service goes on

Starts together, ends apart: that's drift, and it's a *sample-rate mismatch* wearing a mystery costume — a 44.1 kHz device in a 48 kHz world, or a capture device buffering under load. The fix is upstream of any sync slider.

Deep dives: [why audio drifts by the end of the sermon](/kb/articles/obs-audio-drift-long-services/), and [audio/video sync fixes](/kb/troubleshooting/audio-delay-video-stream-fix/).

## 7. The lips and the sound are offset — by the same amount, all service

Constant offset is latency, not drift: the video path (cameras, switcher, NDI) takes longer than the audio path. Measure it once with a clap on camera, delay the *audio* to match the slowest video, write the number down where the next operator will find it.

Deep dives: [audio and video out of sync](/kb/troubleshooting/audio-delay-video-stream-fix/), and [camera video lagging behind audio](/kb/troubleshooting/camera-lagging-behind-audio-stream/).

## 8. The stream mix sounds nothing like the room

Speech buried, band enormous, prayers inaudible — because the stream got the *house* mix, and the house mix leans on a physical room the stream can't hear. The structural fix: a dedicated aux or bus mix for the stream, balanced in headphones against what the platform actually receives, owned by someone who checks it weekly.

Deep dives: [routing a proper stream mix from your mixer](/kb/software/obs/obs-audio-routing-church-mixer/), the [X32 stream-audio setup](/kb/mixers/behringer/behringer-x32-streaming-audio-obs-vmix/), and the [XR18 aux-over-USB recipe](/kb/mixers/behringer/behringer-xr18-streaming-aux-usb/).

## 9. Wireless mics drop out mid-sentence

The most theatrical failure and the most preventable: batteries below half, antennas behind a metal rack, or a frequency newly contested since someone's gear moved in nearby. The habits beat the heroics — batteries at 75%+ before every service, line-of-sight antennas, a frequency scan whenever dropouts cluster, and spares staged where the pastor can reach them.

Deep dive: the [maintenance rhythm that keeps consumables honest](/kb/articles/av-inventory-maintenance/).

## 10. It worked last Sunday

A scene didn't recall, a guest engineer "fixed" the gain structure, phantom power got switched off a channel that needs it, the master was muted during teardown and nobody noticed. Digital consoles make this survivable: a known-good scene saved and *actually recalled* pre-service is the audio version of a settings baseline.

Deep dives: [scene recall that volunteers can trust](/kb/mixers/behringer/behringer-x32-scene-recall-volunteers/), and [training volunteers on the workflow, not folklore](/kb/articles/training-volunteer-av-teams/).
