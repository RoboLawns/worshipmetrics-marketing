## You're not the problem — the scrubbing is

If you run your church's social media, you already know the platforms cold. You know a Reel needs to hook in the first second, that captions matter because everyone watches muted, that consistency beats perfection. None of that is your bottleneck.

Your bottleneck is Monday: a 75-minute service recording, somewhere inside it a 30-second moment that would genuinely reach someone scrolling on a Tuesday — and the only way to find it, historically, is to *re-watch the whole thing.* That hour you don't have is why the clips get sporadic, then stop. The problem was never your skill or your will. It was the scrubbing, and the scrubbing is the part that's now solved.

## The workflow, Sunday afternoon

Here's the actual sequence with [Worship Clips](/solutions/worship-clips/), and notice how little of it is "editing":

1. **The recording is already there.** If your church [streams through the platform](/live-streaming/), every service records automatically — no "did someone grab the file?" Even moments your booth operator flagged live show up as clip sources.
2. **AI hands you a ranked list, not a blank timeline.** The service is transcribed (with church vocabulary, so "Habakkuk" isn't "have a duck"), and each candidate moment arrives with a **shareability score (0–100)**, a category (sermon point, worship, prayer, testimony…), and a suggested **hook line**. You review the *top of a ranked list* — the machinery behind that scoring is detailed in [how AI finds your sermon moments](/kb/articles/how-ai-finds-sermon-moments/).
3. **You do the human part — taste.** Pick the moment that fits your church's voice, tweak the caption from the suggested draft, trim if you want. This is where a *human* social media person is irreplaceable, and it's exactly the part you're good at.
4. **Every format renders itself.** Your pick comes out in **9:16 vertical** (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, Stories), **1:1 square** (feed), and **16:9 widescreen** (YouTube, website) — captions burned in, your branding applied. No exporting three times.
5. **Publish.** Finished clips [publish directly to YouTube](/kb/articles/sunday-sermon-instagram-before-monday/); for Instagram and TikTok you download the vertical render and post from your phone. Thirty seconds, done.

The whole loop is a couple of hours a week, not an all-day edit — which is the difference between a cadence you can *sustain* and the burnout that ends most church social accounts.

## The catalog that makes you look organized

One underrated gift for the person who runs the account: every clip lives in a **library organized by service**, showing what's been made and what's been posted. That means you can plan — pull a worship moment for Thursday, save a testimony for the campaign next month, resurface last Easter's best clip this Easter. You stop scrambling for "something to post today" because you're working from a stocked shelf instead of a blank feed. (That catalog is also a leadership asset — more on that in [reaching the next generation with short video](/kb/articles/reach-young-people-short-video-church/).)

## When you want backup — or a night off

Even a streamlined workflow benefits from a second person, and the good news is the skill floor is low: someone who can tell a good moment from a flat one can own the review-and-post step, freeing you for strategy or a week off. If you'd like to train that person, [training a church clips volunteer](/kb/articles/train-church-video-clip-volunteer/) is written for exactly that hand-off. And the moment-finding AI means you're never training someone to *edit* — only to *choose*, which anyone with taste can learn in an afternoon.

You already had the hardest parts — the eye, the platform sense, the heart for reaching people. What was missing was the two hours nobody has on a Monday. Now the service hands you the moments, and you get to do the part only a human can.
