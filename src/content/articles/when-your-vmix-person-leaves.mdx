## The resignation letter every tech booth eventually gets

It arrives as good news, usually. A job offer in another city. A house closer to family. A season of life that finally demands the Sundays back. Your vMix person — the one who built the booth, knows its moods, and has run it alone for years — is moving on, with love and six weeks' notice.

And now the church discovers what it actually had. Not a volunteer with a skill: **a single point of failure with a personality.** The gear stays; what leaves is everything the gear doesn't know about itself — which port feeds the confidence monitor, why input four is named that, what the fix was the last time the stream died during communion. If that knowledge lives only in the departing head, the six weeks become a frantic brain-dump and the first month after becomes a series of small archaeologies.

Here's the succession plan that avoids that — and the honest part is that every piece of it is worth doing *now*, whether or not anyone ever resigns.

## Move 1: get the knowledge out of the head and into the system

The difference between a smooth handoff and a crisis is almost entirely **where the knowledge lives**. Three homes for it:

- **The [Signal Map](/system-documentation/)** documents every device, port, and labeled cable run — so "which cable feeds the confidence monitor" is a lookup, not a memory. It prints, too: a wiring diagram and cable-by-cable patch sheet, stamped with the moment they were made. If your gear list is a whiteboard photo, this is the single most valuable thing your current tech person can do in their remaining tenure. (Connect a [UniFi controller](/kb/articles/connect-unifi-worshipmetrics/) and the network layer documents itself.)
- **The support history** is institutional memory nobody has to write: every self-healing action, every alert, every remote intervention, timestamped. The next person inherits not just a system but its *medical records* — what fails, how often, what fixed it.
- **The [health reports](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/)** carry the baselines: what a good Sunday looks like on this network with these settings. The successor doesn't have to rediscover normal; it's on file.

Notice what all three share: they accumulate automatically once set up. Documentation that depends on discipline dies with the person who had it. Documentation the system keeps is succession-proof.

## Move 2: succeed to a team, not a replacement

The instinct is to search for "another vMix person" — one unicorn to replace the old one. The better answer is the one running through [this whole series](/kb/articles/you-already-have-vmix/): the job that's leaving was never one job. Split it into seats — lyrics, cameras, the [clips queue](/kb/articles/sunday-sermon-instagram-before-monday/), monitoring, the switcher — and suddenly you're not recruiting a wizard; you're [asking several ordinary people for one small yes each](/kb/articles/recruit-church-tech-volunteers/). The [training machinery](/kb/articles/train-vmix-operators-without-risking-sunday/) — draft runs, the mentor panel, telemetry debriefs — turns the outgoing person's final weeks into a teaching residency instead of a panic.

And if the deadline is real and the bench is thin, remember [where the bench actually is](/kb/articles/need-volunteers-church-livestream/): the youth ministry, one specific invitation away.

## Move 3: build the bridges for the gap

Between the goodbye and the fully-trained team, three bridges hold weight:

- **The automation floor.** [Self-healing](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) doesn't resign. Dropped streams restart, stopped recordings recover, and [alerts reach whoever's on call](/kb/articles/church-stream-drop-phone-alert/) — the routine failures stay handled while humans reorganize.
- **The alum on the panel.** Here's the gentle secret of [cloud control](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/): moving away no longer means leaving the team. Your departed vMix person — still invited, still trusted — can watch preview and program from their new city and coach the rookie through the big Easter service. Many alumni *want* this; nobody builds a booth for years without loving it. Let the goodbye be a change of seat, not an exit.
- **The professional floor.** With the Sunday Assurance add-on, a production pro from the WorshipMetrics team is on call every Sunday and can see your system — the real Signal Map, the real support history — the moment you call [910-WORSHIP](/phone-support/). The gap between tech directors stops being a free-fall.

## The churches that never have this crisis

Some churches read this post as a fire drill. The wiser read is a design principle: the churches that shrug off a departure are the ones where succession was always running — documentation accumulating by default, [a ladder of students](/kb/articles/start-church-av-club-vmix/) climbing seats, older volunteers training younger ones, and [the booth understood as a ministry](/kb/articles/tech-booth-youth-ministry/) that produces people rather than consuming one.

In those churches, the vMix person still leaves someday — for the new city, the new season, with the send-off they've earned. The difference is what they leave behind: not a hole shaped exactly like themselves, but a team they trained, records that remember, and a fifteen-year-old on camera two who already knows who *she's* going to train next.
