## 4:30 p.m. — coffee, laptop, ninety minutes

*A composite Tuesday, drawn from how the most relaxed tech directors we know actually spend it.*

Dave runs AV at a church of about two hundred, on top of a day job. His secret isn't heroics — it's that he stopped doing Sunday-morning firefighting and started doing Tuesday-afternoon reading. Ninety minutes, same order every week.

## Minute 0: the alert feed — reading the silence

First stop is the [Pulse](/av-monitoring/) alert feed. Most Tuesdays it's short, and that brevity is trustworthy because the [thresholds are published](/kb/articles/pulse-alert-thresholds-reference/): he knows exactly what would have fired an alert, so an empty feed is evidence, not hope.

This week there's one entry: a critical RAM alert on the booth PC, Sunday, 9:38 a.m., auto-resolved by 10:15. Nobody noticed anything in the room. But Dave has read [why streams degrade forty minutes in](/kb/articles/livestream-stutters-40-minutes-in/), and one RAM excursion is how that story starts. He makes a note: find what's eating memory before it becomes a pattern. The feed keeps 90 days of history, so next Tuesday he'll know if it was a fluke or a trend.

## Minute 20: the self-healing log — the booth's confession

Next, the [self-healing log](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) in the support history. This is the report card most churches never see: everything the booth fixed without asking. This week — nothing. Three weeks ago it was a stream restart during a storm; the log showed it, the [drop alert](/kb/articles/church-stream-drop-phone-alert/) reached his phone, and the incident was over before he finished reading it.

Dave's rule of thumb comes straight from experience: **a booth that self-heals once a quarter is fine; one that self-heals weekly is filing a complaint.** The log is how the complaint gets heard on a Tuesday instead of shouted on a Sunday.

## Minute 30: the physical layer — what the network witnessed

The church runs UniFi, and the [controller is connected](/kb/articles/connect-unifi-worshipmetrics/), so Dave's documentation audits itself. He scans two things:

- **Negotiated link speeds.** Every port that should be gigabit, is — except one. The stage-left run is negotiating 100 Mb/s behind a green link light, which almost always means a damaged pair. That cable was re-pulled during last month's stage rework. Fifteen minutes with a crimper this Saturday, instead of [an NDI mystery](/kb/articles/church-wifi-good-enough-to-livestream/) some future Sunday.
- **PoE draw.** The rear camera is pulling what it always pulls. The lobby access point crept up a watt — noted, not alarming.

Then a glance at the [Signal Map](/system-documentation/): the auto-inferred Ethernet wires still match what he believes about his own building. When they disagree, the switch is usually right.

## Minute 50: disk, recordings, and the archive

Sunday's service recorded automatically, so the [library](/live-streaming/) has it — and Sunday's disk-space trend shows the drive crossing 40% free, gigabytes lower than last month. Recordings accumulate; that's their job. He archives two months of raw services to the external drive before the [10%-free cleanup rule](/kb/articles/booth-pc-crashes-mid-service/) ever has to earn its keep. Storage planning on a Tuesday costs nothing.

## Minute 60: people, not machines

The last half hour is the human layer. Next month's [volunteer grid](/kb/articles/scheduling-volunteers-month-at-a-time/) needs drafting: he fills the stream seat, camera, and lyrics positions across four Sundays — silently, no emails firing while he thinks — checks the blockout warnings, and saves the draft to sleep on. Thursday he'll hit Send once, and [the loop runs itself](/kb/articles/volunteer-scheduling-without-text-scramble/): one-tap accepts, decline notifications, day-before reminders.

He also spends ten minutes on next quarter's ask. The health reports show bitrate sagging during greeting time three weeks running — the classic guest-WiFi squeeze. Three weeks of five-second telemetry make a better [budget case](/kb/articles/av-budget-planning-churches/) for a network upgrade than any hunch ever could.

## 6:00 p.m. — done

Laptop closed. Nothing about this Tuesday was urgent, which is precisely the point: every item was a Sunday problem intercepted at the cheap end of its life. The [service director's seat](/av-team/service-director/) gets the glory on Sunday morning — but the calm it enjoys was manufactured here, in ninety unglamorous minutes with a coffee.

Sundays are won on Tuesdays. The telemetry just makes the winning legible.
