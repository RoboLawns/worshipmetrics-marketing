## Count your logins

Do a quick inventory of what your church pays for to get through a Sunday. A typical list:

- A **streaming or restreaming** service, to reach YouTube and Facebook.
- A **clipping tool**, if anyone's making sermon shorts.
- **Presentation and lyrics** software.
- A **volunteer scheduling** app.
- A **chat** app or group texts to coordinate the team.
- **Monitoring and documentation** — usually nothing, a gap you're just absorbing.

That's five or six subscriptions, five or six logins, five or six bills renewing on five or six dates — and, worst of all, five or six tools that **don't talk to each other.** The setlist gets typed into presentation software, then again into the stream's lower-thirds, then referenced by hand for the clip titles. The schedule lives one place, the chat about the schedule another. Every seam between tools is a place where a volunteer gets lost and work gets redone.

This post is the case for closing the seams — not because bundling is trendy, but because for a *volunteer-run church specifically*, the disconnection is the actual tax.

## The hidden costs of a scattered stack

The subscription total is the visible cost. Three invisible ones usually hurt more:

- **The volunteer friction tax.** Six tools means six accounts a new volunteer needs, six interfaces to learn, six places something can be "set wrong." The [scattered stack is quietly why recruiting and training are so hard](/kb/articles/nobody-else-qualified-church-tech/) — you're not onboarding someone to a role, you're onboarding them to a software collection.
- **The re-entry tax.** Data typed more than once is data that drifts and time that's wasted. When the [service plan](/services/) is the single source and the [slides](/kb/articles/ccli-reporting-with-lyrics-software/), the stream, and the clips all read from it, the re-typing — and the mismatches — disappear.
- **The nobody-can-see-it-all tax.** When something breaks, no single tool (and no support line behind any of them) can see the whole picture. Six vendors each know their slice; none knows your *system*. That's the gap [context-aware support](/kb/articles/ai-that-knows-your-wiring/) exists to close — and it's only possible when the pieces live together.

## The DIY church's platform

One distinction worth naming, because it's who this is for: consolidation here doesn't mean *outsourcing*. WorshipMetrics isn't a production company you hand your service to — it's the platform for the church that wants its **own** volunteers running its **own** booth, with everything they need in one place instead of six. The cloud layer, the monitoring, the support: all of it exists to make a *self-run* ministry more capable, not to replace it. That's a different promise than "let us do it for you," and it's the right one for churches that see their tech team as a ministry, not a line to eliminate.

## Start where it hurts

You don't consolidate in a weekend, and you shouldn't. Most churches start with the single piece causing the most pain — the streaming that keeps dropping, the clips nobody has time to cut, the monitoring gap that turned into a lost recording — feel the difference of having it *connected* to everything else, and fold in the rest [as renewals come up](/pricing/). One platform, reached at your own pace, until the day you realize the Sunday stack is one login, one bill, and one number to call.

Six subscriptions was never the plan. It's just what happened one tool at a time. This is how it un-happens.
