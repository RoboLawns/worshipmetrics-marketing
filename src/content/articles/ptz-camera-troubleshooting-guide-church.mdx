## How to use this guide when the service starts in twenty minutes

This is a field guide, not a lecture. Scan the ten symptoms below, find the one on your screen, and run its fast checks **in order** — each links to a full deep dive if the quick version doesn't land the fix. Mid-service, remember the camera operator's version of first aid: **switch to another angle first**, then troubleshoot. Two rules make every fix safer: change one thing at a time, and say what you touched.

This guide is part of a series — the booth's software front doors are [OBS](/kb/articles/obs-troubleshooting-guide-church/), [vMix](/kb/articles/vmix-troubleshooting-guide-church/), and [ProPresenter](/kb/articles/propresenter-troubleshooting-guide-church/) troubleshooting, and the pre-service half of all of them lives on one [printable checklist](/sunday-checklist/).

## 1. The camera won't connect to the network at all

Follow the physical layer before touching software: link lights at both ends, the same cable into a known-good port, and — for PoE cameras — whether the camera is getting power at all (no LED means no PoE, and that's a switch conversation, not a camera one). If the hardware path is alive, the question becomes addressing: is the camera even on the same subnet as the machine looking for it?

Deep dives: [PTZ camera not connecting, step by step](/kb/troubleshooting/ptz-camera-not-connecting-network/), and [church network design for AV](/kb/articles/church-network-av-streaming/).

## 2. The camera keeps losing its IP address

The Sunday classic: everything worked until the building's router rebooted, and now the camera answers at a new address nobody knows. DHCP is the culprit — a camera that other gear points at by address needs a **static IP or a DHCP reservation**, chosen once, written down, and labeled on the camera itself.

Deep dive: [why PTZ cameras lose their IP, and the permanent fix](/kb/troubleshooting/ptz-camera-losing-ip-address/).

## 3. An NDI camera vanished from the source list

NDI discovery is a network feature, so a missing NDI camera is a network symptom: same subnet and VLAN (mDNS discovery doesn't cross VLANs by default), no firewall that reclassified the network as "Public," and the camera's NDI stream actually enabled. The software's source list is the last suspect.

Deep dives: [NDI camera not showing in OBS or vMix](/kb/troubleshooting/ndi-camera-not-showing-obs-vmix/), and [NDI on a church network, fixed end to end](/kb/troubleshooting/ndi-not-working-church-network/).

## 4. The camera's web UI won't load

You can see its video but not its settings page — usually a browser problem wearing a camera costume: an HTTPS redirect the camera can't serve, a cached page from an old firmware, or a plugin-era interface that wants a different browser. Try plain `http://`, an incognito window, and a second browser before blaming the camera.

Deep dive: [camera web UI not loading in the browser](/kb/troubleshooting/avkans-camera-web-ui-not-loading/).

## 5. Presets recall to the wrong position

The wide shot is suddenly framing the drummer's elbow. Presets drift for physical reasons — the camera was nudged, the mount loosened, power cycled mid-move — and for digital ones: the preset was re-saved by accident, or different control surfaces are numbering presets differently. Re-save from a known-good framing and label which surface owns the presets.

Deep dive: [PTZ presets not recalling correctly](/kb/troubleshooting/ptz-preset-not-recalling-correctly/).

## 6. The image flickers or pulses under the lights

Flicker is almost never the camera failing — it's the camera's shutter arguing with LED lighting. The fix is matching the camera's anti-flicker/frequency setting to the room's lighting, and on dimmer-driven LEDs, moving the dimmer off the flicker-inducing range. Network congestion produces a different artifact — stutter — with a different fix.

Deep dive: [camera flickering and stuttering during church streams](/kb/troubleshooting/camera-image-flickering-church/).

## 7. The video lags behind the audio

The room hears the downbeat, the stream sees it late. Camera paths (especially NDI and heavily-processed inputs) add frames of delay that the audio path doesn't share. Measure the offset once, delay the *audio* to match the slowest camera, and write the number where the next operator will find it.

Deep dives: [camera video lagging behind audio](/kb/troubleshooting/camera-lagging-behind-audio-stream/), and [audio/video sync fixes](/kb/troubleshooting/audio-delay-video-stream-fix/).

## 8. The picture went black mid-service

For HDMI and SDI cameras, a mid-service black frame is a *path* problem: a marginal cable finally failing, an extender or converter overheating, or an EDID handshake renegotiating. Switch angles, then test the path segment by segment after service — the failure is almost always at a connector, not inside the camera.

Deep dives: [HDMI signal dropping or going black](/kb/troubleshooting/hdmi-signal-dropping-church-setup/), and [which AV signals survive which paths](/kb/articles/av-signals-that-survive-network-switch/).

## 9. Control works but video doesn't — or the reverse

A PTZ camera is two systems in one body: a video path (SDI, HDMI, or NDI) and a control path (VISCA over the network, or serial). They fail independently — which is diagnostic gold. Joystick works but no picture? The video path is the patient. Picture fine but no control? Check the control port, protocol, and address in your controller's config.

Deep dive: [the full PTZ setup guide — video and control paths explained](/kb/articles/ptz-camera-setup-church/).

## 10. It worked last Sunday

Nothing changed — except the switch got two more devices plugged in and its **PoE budget** ran out at the exact moment every camera woke up. Or a port died quietly. Or someone re-patched a cable and didn't say so. Power budgets, port health, and undocumented changes are the three ghosts of camera-land; all three die in daylight.

Deep dives: [PoE classes and power budgets for church AV](/kb/articles/poe-classes-church-av-guide/), and [an AV inventory that stays true](/kb/articles/av-inventory-maintenance/).

## When the list stops helping: three upgrades that change the game

If this page fixed your morning, bookmark it and go do the service. But cameras are network devices bolted to walls — the fixes above all assumed you knew which switch port, which cable, and which address. Those assumptions are upgradeable.

### Upgrade one: a mapped system is a system you can hand to someone else

Every camera fix above starts with "follow the signal backward" — thirty seconds with a real map, half a service without one. A [Signal Map](/system-documentation/) — every camera, every labeled cable, every switch port, every address, printable as patch sheets — turns troubleshooting into wayfinding and **makes the knowledge transferable**: new volunteers train against the map, a [labeling discipline](/kb/articles/cable-labeling-system-that-survives/) survives re-racks, and the booth survives its builder leaving.

### Upgrade two: put every camera on watch

Here's the camera-specific superpower: stop discovering failures on Sunday. Flag a camera "Alert when offline" in WorshipMetrics and every machine running [Tech Manager](/tech-manager/) probes it continuously — and because [multiple machines act as witnesses](/kb/articles/multi-observer-device-health/), [Pulse](/av-monitoring/) can tell you the difference that matters: *the camera is down* (every observer agrees — walk to the booth) versus *one path to the camera is down* (verdict: degraded — check a switch port, not the camera). A camera that dies Thursday night pages your phone Thursday night, with [published thresholds](/kb/articles/pulse-alert-thresholds-reference/), not vibes.

[Luminos](/luminos-ai-assistant/), the AI assistant in WorshipMetrics, doesn't answer camera questions from generic checklists — [it reads your church](/kb/articles/ai-that-knows-your-wiring/): your inventory, your Signal Map down to cable IDs and switch ports, live device health from the watchers above, and every past incident.
