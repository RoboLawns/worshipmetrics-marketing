## The short answer: yes — and it changes what "the vMix seat" means

vMix runs on one PC, so churches naturally assume it's a one-person instrument: whoever sits at that keyboard runs the service, and everyone else watches. With WorshipMetrics connected, that stops being true. **The booth's switching surface becomes something your team shares** — locally for free on every plan, and from anywhere on Assist and Stream — while vMix itself stays exactly where it is, doing exactly what it does.

Concretely, here's what a second (or third, or fifth) person gets, on any device they sign into:

- **Preview and program, live** — thumbnails of every input beside an enlargeable program view, so anyone with access sees exactly what's on air and what's queued next. No more describing the screen over the phone.
- **The full switch** — cut, fade, wipe, zoom, and stinger transitions with adjustable duration, a hands-on T-bar, and preview/program switching across every input, through vMix's own HTTP and TCP APIs.
- **The everyday moves** — play and pause inputs, toggle overlays 1–4, manage bus audio mute and volume.
- **Honest audio meters** — master and bus levels streamed at ~30 fps over a persistent TCP connection: the same numbers vMix shows, live in the panel.
- **Stream and recording control** — start and stop both from the panel, which means a second person can *confirm the red light is actually on* before the prelude ends.

And the two facts that make a tech director comfortable handing any of this out: there's **no port forwarding and no VPN** (one secure outbound connection from the booth, the same link that powers [monitoring](/av-monitoring/)), and **every remote action is logged** to your support history. You decide who can switch; the system remembers who did.

## What churches actually do with a shared surface

The feature list matters less than the Sundays it changes. Four patterns come up constantly:

**The confirmation seat.** The cheapest insurance in church production: someone — at the back of the room, at home, at another campus — glances at the panel and confirms the stream is live, the recording is rolling, and the right input is in program. Thirty seconds, every week, and an entire category of "we didn't notice until Monday" disappears.

**The remote mentor.** Your veteran operator is at a wedding; a nervous newcomer is in the booth. The veteran opens the panel, watches preview and program through the service, and texts one line: *"you're doing great."* When needed, they talk the newcomer through a transition — or make the switch themselves. We told this story from the newcomer's side in the [first-Sunday stream operator diary](/kb/articles/diary-first-sunday-stream-operator/), and from the church's side in [can volunteers run the livestream when the tech director is out of town?](/kb/articles/volunteers-run-livestream-without-tech-director/)

**The sick-Sunday save.** The scheduled operator wakes up with the flu. A veteran volunteer takes the whole service remotely — real switching, not a locked-down viewer — while a warm body in the building handles the physical room. What used to be a cancelled stream is now a normal Sunday with an unusual seating chart.

**The training split.** This is the quiet superpower for growing teams: the trainee sits at the vMix keyboard where the tactile learning happens, while the trainer watches the *same live surface* from the next seat or the next town — seeing every preview choice before it hits program. Mistakes get caught in preview, which is exactly where mistakes are supposed to live. It's the pattern that makes [putting students on the switcher](/kb/articles/students-run-church-livestream-safely/) genuinely safe, and it's how one operator's knowledge stops bottlenecking and starts multiplying — the heart of [the team your vMix system can carry](/kb/articles/you-already-have-vmix/).

## What this doesn't change

vMix keeps its scene collections, its inputs, its show file, its keyboard shortcuts — the operator's craft transfers untouched, because WorshipMetrics speaks to vMix through its own APIs rather than replacing anything. The booth PC stays the engine; the panel extends its reach. And local control through [Tech Manager](/tech-manager/) costs nothing, forever, so the shared-surface habit can start today even on the Free plan — the cloud layer joins when the team is ready for it.
