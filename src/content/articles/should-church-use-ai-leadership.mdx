## The question deserves better than a verdict

Somewhere this year, your board will discuss AI — if [Barna's numbers](https://www.barna.com/research/pastors-using-ai-ministry/) hold, most of the room will already be using it quietly (only 13% of pastors abstain entirely) while most of the room also feels uneasy about it (71% express hesitation). That combination — adoption plus unease — is exactly when leadership frameworks earn their keep, because the alternative is drift: a hundred small AI decisions made by whoever got there first, with no shared line and no shared language.

Here's a framework built for that meeting: three zones, five vendor questions, and one worked example. It doesn't tell your church what to believe; it gives the room a way to disagree precisely instead of anxiously.

## The three zones

**Zone one: technical and operational — where AI clearly serves.** Equipment support, [system monitoring](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/), scheduling logistics, troubleshooting guidance. Work with objective right answers, no spiritual authority, and enormous volunteer pain behind it. This is where pastors already use AI most — behind the scenes — and where the case is strongest: an AI that [knows your actual wiring](/kb/articles/ai-that-knows-your-wiring/) answering a volunteer's question [at 8:45 on a Sunday](/kb/articles/sunday-morning-church-tech-support/) is serving the church the way a hymnal or a soundboard serves it. Our whole position on this zone: [AI for the booth, not the pulpit](/kb/articles/ai-in-the-church-booth-not-pulpit/).

**Zone two: public-facing content — proceed with guardrails.** Social clips, graphics, summaries, drafts. Useful, but it carries the church's voice, so the guardrail is non-negotiable: **AI proposes, humans decide.** (Worked example: [the sermon-clips pipeline](/kb/articles/how-ai-finds-sermon-moments/) ranks moments and suggests captions; a person chooses what represents your church.)

**Zone three: preaching, prayer, pastoral care — conviction territory.** This is where [pastors themselves draw the line](https://www.barna.com/research/church-leaders-ai-usage-concerns/), and where your church's theology, not a vendor's roadmap, should rule. Some churches will use AI for sermon *research*; others won't let it near the study. Leadership's job here isn't to settle the question today — it's to *name the zone* so nobody wanders into it by accident, and to choose partners who respect the line rather than blur it.

## The five vendor questions

When anyone offers your church AI, these five questions sort the serious from the shiny:

1. **"What context does your AI actually have about us?"** Generic AI gives generic answers. The useful kind has read your systems — [your inventory, your Signal Map, your live telemetry](/system-documentation/). If the vendor can't say what the AI knows, it knows nothing.
2. **"Where does our data live?"** Ask specifically about sensitive media. (Design worth looking for: WorshipMetrics' clip pipeline offers a [local mode](/kb/articles/how-ai-finds-sermon-moments/) where service audio and video never leave the booth computer — only short text goes to the cloud for scoring.)
3. **"Does the AI act, or propose?"** Autonomous AI in a church system should worry you. The right pattern: routine, reversible fixes automated *and logged*; everything else proposed to a human. ([Self-healing's own restraint](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) — a crashed app is reported, never silently relaunched — is what this looks like in practice.)
4. **"Who stands behind it when AI isn't enough?"** AI without a human ladder is an answering machine with confidence. Look for real escalation: [weekday pros, Sunday on-call, a number that answers](/kb/articles/church-av-tech-support-options/).
5. **"Who built this — and do they understand church?"** Not as a gotcha; as due diligence. Tools built by people who serve in churches carry the zones in their bones. WorshipMetrics is a pastor, an engineer, and an AV educator, all integrally involved in their own congregations — and Paul still answers 910-WORSHIP himself. That biography is why the pulpit line holds by design, not by policy memo.

## A reasonable first step for a cautious board

Don't start with a debate; start with a bounded experiment in zone one. The [free tier](/kb/articles/small-church-tech-support-budget/) includes smart help inside alerts and one full AI session — enough for your tech team to judge, against a real problem, whether AI-with-context is what this framework claims. Review it at the next meeting with evidence instead of vibes: the [health reports](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/) will show exactly what the season looked like.

Leadership's calling here isn't to bless or ban a technology. It's to put each use in its right zone, guard the zones that matter most, and let the booth — [and the volunteers carrying it](/kb/articles/will-ai-replace-church-tech-volunteers/) — receive the help that's finally affordable. The pulpit stays yours. It was never for sale.
