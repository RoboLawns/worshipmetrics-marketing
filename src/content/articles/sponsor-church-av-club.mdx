## To the one who kept the sound on

Every church has a few of you. You ran the board through three pastors. You pulled cable through the attic in August. You remember when the "new" system was new — because you installed it. Or maybe you never touched the booth, but you've reached the season of giving where one question matters more than any other: *will this outlive me in the lives of young people?*

This post is an invitation built for you. Your church's tech booth is quietly becoming one of the best youth ministries in the building — students learning real crafts, serving the whole congregation, mentored by adults, [staying connected to the church](/kb/articles/students-who-stay-serving-retention/) through the exact years most young people drift. What that ministry needs is what every ministry needs: **someone of an earlier generation to put foundations under it.** Tools, and — if you're willing — time.

## What your gift actually funds

The [AV Club model](/av-club/) runs on structure: each student owns one seat (camera, lyrics, audio, stream, recording), mentors watch and coach, rotations are scheduled a month ahead, and the whole system — training, safety nets, documentation, scheduling — rides on the WorshipMetrics platform. The platform is what makes handing teenagers real responsibility [safe by design](/kb/articles/students-run-church-livestream-safely/): dropped streams restart themselves, mentors see a student's live controls from anywhere, and every service produces a health report that turns Sunday into curriculum.

The mechanics of sponsoring it are deliberately simple, and deliberately church-shaped:

- **You give to your church, not to a company.** Use your church's normal giving — online, check, or cash — designated *"Gift — WorshipMetrics subscription."* It's a charitable contribution like any other.
- **One annual gift, not a subscription in your name.** The annual plan covers a full year — every service, special event, and holiday — with two months free built in. Nobody defends a monthly line item at budget meetings; the ministry just *has* its tools.
- **The church signs up; the team gets started.** Full details, plan tiers, and the plain-English giving notes live at [worshipmetrics.com/gift](/gift/).

Some sponsors give this in honor or memory of someone — the parent who taught them electronics, the tech director who first let them touch the board. A tools-for-the-next-generation gift carries that kind of name well.

## The other half of the gift: your Tuesdays

Money buys the tools. It cannot buy what else you have. Twenty years of judgment — how the room behaves when it's full, why that mic placement, how to stay calm when something dies at 10:58 — exists in your head and nowhere else, and the [booth is one of the only rooms in church life](/kb/articles/intergenerational-ministry-tech-booth/) where handing it down happens naturally: veteran and student, side by side, same headset channel, every week.

The mentoring is lighter-duty than you may fear. Modern systems mean you're not chained to the booth as the safety net — you can [watch a student's actual controls from home](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/) and coach with a text, and the [routine failures fix themselves](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/). Veterans who've done it tell us the same thing, almost word for word: *training a student made the booth feel like ministry again.* Your knowledge stops bottlenecking and starts multiplying — that's [the succession story](/kb/articles/when-your-vmix-person-leaves/), solved a decade before it's urgent.

## How to start the conversation at your church

If your church already has students in the booth, the path is short: tell the tech director or pastor you'd like to sponsor the platform for a year, and point them at [the gift page](/gift/). If the club doesn't exist yet, you may be the person who causes it: hand your pastor [the one-page AV Club proposal](/av-club/proposal/) — what it is, what it produces, what it requires — with a sentence that tends to move rooms: *"I'll fund the first year."*

## What it looks like a decade out

Sponsorships like this don't produce plaques. They produce people: the girl who learned cameras at fourteen running production at her college church; the boy who mixed his first service under your eye now training his own students; a booth that never again depends on one exhausted volunteer, because a generation was built instead of borrowed.

You kept the sound on for twenty years. This is how it stays on for twenty more — with your name quietly inside every clean Sunday, whether or not anyone ever says it aloud.
