## Two ministries, praying for each other's answer

Walk your church's hallways on a Wednesday and you'll find two leaders carrying mirror-image burdens, usually without ever comparing notes.

In one room: the media/AV lead, [running on fumes](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/), praying for *people* — anyone teachable, anyone faithful, anyone who might take a seat before the [next resignation letter](/kb/articles/when-your-vmix-person-leaves/) writes itself.

In the other: the youth pastor, rich in people and praying for *substance* — some form of real responsibility to disciple with, something beyond another game night, something that makes students [needed instead of entertained](/kb/articles/youth-group-beyond-game-night/), something that might keep them connected [past graduation](/kb/articles/students-who-stay-serving-retention/).

Each leader's surplus is the other's shortage. The media team has real jobs and no people; the youth ministry has people and no real jobs. Kept separate, both problems are chronic. Connected, they annihilate each other. That connection has a name — a pipeline — and building it is less work than either leader is currently spending on their half of the problem.

## The pipeline, drawn plainly

**From the youth ministry flows:** named students, matched to seats by gifting (the [seat finder](/tools/church-av-seat-finder/) makes this a two-minute conversation); parental trust, because the invitation comes through a ministry families already know; and the discipleship framing — this is serving, not free labor, and the youth pastor keeps saying so.

**From the media team flows:** the seats themselves — [seven real roles](/kb/articles/serving-opportunities-teens-without-stage/) the congregation depends on; the training, made cheap by modern structure ([one seat at a time, draft-run rehearsals, mentors on the live panel](/kb/articles/train-vmix-operators-without-risking-sunday/)); and the ladder — tablet to lyrics to camera to switcher, volunteer to trainer.

**Underneath both, the shared machinery** that keeps the pipeline from consuming either leader: [self-healing systems](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) that make student Sundays [safe by design](/kb/articles/students-run-church-livestream-safely/), [month-ahead scheduling](/kb/articles/volunteer-scheduling-without-text-scramble/) with blockouts students manage themselves, and mentor visibility [from anywhere](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/). The [AV Club](/kb/articles/start-church-av-club-vmix/) is the pipeline's visible form — the name, the monthly rhythm, the pizza, the culture of older students training younger ones that eventually makes the whole thing self-sustaining.

## What each leader gets back

**The media lead** gets the thing no recruiting announcement ever delivered: a *continuous supply* of teachable people — plus the unexpected gift veterans keep reporting, that [mentoring a student made the booth feel like ministry again](/kb/articles/intergenerational-ministry-tech-booth/). The [burnout cycle](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/) breaks and stays broken, because the pipeline replaces every graduate.

**The youth pastor** gets discipleship infrastructure they didn't have to build: real responsibility, adult mentors outside the youth staff, visible growth, public recognition — [ministry *by* teenagers](/kb/articles/ministry-by-teenagers-tech-booth/) with the risk already engineered out. And a retention story: students with roles have [something habit-proof](/kb/articles/students-who-stay-serving-retention/) when the routine breaks at 18.

**The church** gets the compounding returns: a booth that [survives any single departure](/kb/articles/when-your-vmix-person-leaves/), one of the only genuinely [intergenerational rooms](/kb/articles/intergenerational-ministry-tech-booth/) in the building, and — quietly, over years — its next generation of tech directors, grown instead of hunted.

## The one-meeting start

Pipelines fail when they're launched as programs and thrive when they're launched as agreements. So: one meeting, two leaders, two lists. The media lead brings the three most understaffed seats, described in one sentence each. The youth pastor brings the students whose gifts already point at screens, sound, cameras, or detail — the [gamer, the editor, the playlist kid](/kb/articles/teen-gaming-video-skills-church/). Match one pair. Pick a start Sunday. Put the mentor beside them.

Then let the first success do the recruiting — because it will. A student wearing a headset tells their friends; a relieved media lead tells the elders; a parent tells another parent. That's the pipeline priming itself.

Two ministries have been praying at each other across a hallway. Introduce them — and when it's time to bring leadership in, [the printable AV Club proposal](/av-club/proposal/) carries the whole case into the elder meeting on one page.
