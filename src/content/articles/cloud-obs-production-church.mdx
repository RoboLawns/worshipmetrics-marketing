## OBS was built to be driven remotely — most churches just never wired it up

Here's a fact that surprises many church OBS operators: OBS has spoken a remote-control protocol for years. It's called **WebSocket (v5)**, it ships inside the app, and it's the front door the OBS project maintains precisely so other software can see and drive OBS. What most churches lack isn't the capability — it's a church-shaped way to use it safely on a Sunday. That's what cloud OBS production provides.

The model matches [the rest of the ecosystem](/kb/articles/cloud-video-production-for-churches/): **OBS keeps running locally in your booth, free, on the PC you own — and [WorshipMetrics](/obs-integration/) relays its WebSocket control and streams its live state to any device your team signs into.** Local switch, cloud reach. Nothing about your production moves to a rented server.

## What remote OBS control covers — honestly

Straight about the boundary, because honesty is the whole brand here. Cloud OBS control gives your team the **live-operation surface**:

- **Scene switching** — move between your service scenes from anywhere.
- **Stream control** — start and stop the broadcast remotely.
- **Recording control** — start and stop the recording, and [confirm from a phone that it's actually rolling](/kb/articles/obs-recording-unplayable-after-crash/) before the service starts.
- **Live state visible** — current scene, stream and recording status, and [encoder health streaming in every few seconds](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/), so a remote helper *sees* the booth rather than asking about it.

What stays in the booth: deep scene editing — building scenes, adding sources, audio filter surgery. And that's the right boundary, not a limitation: [mid-service settings changes are their own failure category](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/), and every stable booth adopts the same discipline anyway — **operate live from anywhere, edit on Tuesdays at the machine.** (Churches that want a deeper *remote switching* surface — full T-bar, overlays, bus audio — are the ones who eventually [look at vMix](/kb/articles/cloud-vmix-production-church/); for running an OBS service remotely, scenes plus stream plus recording is the job.)

## Why not just host OBS in the cloud?

Because for a church, [local-first wins on the three things that matter](/kb/articles/cloud-video-production-for-churches/): the live switch never depends on your upload, there's no compute rental on top of the PC you already own, and — the OBS-specific bonus — **it's free.** OBS costs nothing, its local control through Tech Manager costs nothing, and that makes cloud OBS production the lowest-cost path to a reachable, monitorable, team-run booth in all of church tech. You add the cloud layer; you never rent the production.

## What it makes possible

The remote surface is the mechanism; the outcomes are the reason:

- **Confirmation without a car.** "Is it recording? Is it live?" answered — and fixed — from a phone, by whoever's on call.
- **A real second operator.** The backup starts the stream and switches to the countdown from their kitchen when the scheduled volunteer is stuck in traffic — so the [rookie at the desk](/kb/articles/diary-first-sunday-stream-operator/) is never alone.
- **Mentoring with eyes, not guesses.** A veteran watches the actual live scene and encoder state while a [student or new volunteer drives](/kb/articles/train-obs-volunteers-church/) — the difference between coaching and interrogation, and the thing that makes [help-from-home actually work](/kb/articles/obs-help-from-home-sunday/).

One secure outbound connection carries all of it — no port forwarding, no VPN — only invited team members connect, and every remote action is logged. The full story of turning one OBS machine into a team is [you already have OBS](/kb/articles/you-already-have-obs/).

## The platform underneath

Cloud OBS control isn't a standalone gadget — it rides on the same platform that [consolidates your streaming, clipping, presentation, scheduling, monitoring, and support](/kb/articles/one-platform-church-tech-stack/) into one login. Your free encoder, made reachable by your whole team, inside one place that also runs the rest of your church tech. That's cloud production the way a church actually wants it: nothing rented that you already own, nothing outsourced that you'd rather do yourselves, and everything finally in one platform.
