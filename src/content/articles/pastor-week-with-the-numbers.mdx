## Monday, 7:50 a.m. — ninety seconds with the dashboard

*A composite week, drawn from the pastors we've watched go from allergic-to-dashboards to quietly reliant on one.*

Pastor Elena's Monday used to start with a text to the tech booth: *"how'd the stream go?"* — a question that put a volunteer on the spot and never produced a real answer. Now it starts with ninety seconds in [Service Metrics](/service-metrics/) before the first meeting.

Yesterday: 178 in the room, 141 peak online, **Total Reach 319**. Stream health score 94 — she doesn't parse telemetry, and [doesn't need to](/kb/articles/pastor-check-sunday-without-asking-tech-team/); a 94 means the online congregation had a smooth morning. Her text to the booth says something different now: *"Saw the 94 — thank you. Anything the team needs?"* Recognition instead of interrogation. The tech lead has mentioned, more than once, how much that change mattered.

## Wednesday, 10:00 a.m. — the follow-up list is already a list

Three first-time guests scanned the [lobby QR code](/kb/articles/church-check-in-without-an-app/) Sunday; one more was checked in by a greeter at the tablet. All four landed in the directory as Guests, timestamped, with the contact details they typed themselves — no connect cards deciphered, nothing retyped.

Elena filters to "Guests added this month," and the hospitality team's Wednesday assignment writes itself. The family that's been [watching the stream for six weeks before ever walking in](/kb/articles/how-many-watched-church-livestream/) — the modern front porch — gets a welcome that knows they're not actually strangers. That thread runs on through [guest follow-up](/church-guest-follow-up-system/), and it started with a poster and a phone camera.

## Thursday, 2:00 p.m. — the sermon travels

Tuesday, the media volunteer had posted a 48-second clip from Sunday's message — the [reels pipeline](/kb/articles/how-ai-finds-sermon-moments/) had flagged the moment, scored it, and rendered it vertical with captions burned in, so the volunteer chose rather than [edited](/kb/articles/sunday-sermon-instagram-before-monday/). Thursday, Elena hears about it in the grocery store: a member's coworker saw it, asked where the church was.

She doesn't run the pipeline and couldn't tell you the render settings. What she knows is that the sermon she preached once on Sunday kept preaching all week.

## Third Thursday, 6:30 p.m. — the board meeting

The agenda item that used to be a feeling — *are we growing?* — is now a chart. Attendance Over Time, in-person and online broken out separately per service type, measured the same way every week [by policy](/kb/articles/online-church-attendance-what-counts/):

- **Easter versus last Easter:** up 9%, and the shape matters — in-room flat, online up sharply.
- **The spring sermon series:** a visible bump that outlasted the series by three weeks.
- **The online congregation:** growing steadily — the homebound members, the family that moved to Colorado, the not-yet-visitors deciding from a distance.

The conversation changes when the shape of the congregation is visible. The board approves the network upgrade the tech director asked for — his case made with [three weeks of bitrate telemetry](/kb/articles/av-budget-planning-churches/) — in four minutes, because for the first time everyone is looking at the same picture.

## What the numbers are for

Elena would tell you she didn't want a dashboard; she wanted to stop guessing. The counting was never the ministry — but every number this week pointed at a person: a volunteer who deserved thanks, four guests who deserved a welcome, a coworker in a grocery-store conversation, a homebound saint who is *still counted*. 

The data didn't replace the shepherding. It told the shepherd where to look.
