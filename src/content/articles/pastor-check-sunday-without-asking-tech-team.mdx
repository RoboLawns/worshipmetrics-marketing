## The short answer: one dashboard answers both Monday questions

Every Monday morning, a pastor quietly wants the answers to two questions: **did Sunday work?** and **are we growing?** In most churches, both answers currently live in other people's heads — the tech director knows whether the stream struggled, an usher has a headcount on a sticky note, and nobody knows how many people worshiped online.

With WorshipMetrics, both answers are on a dashboard before the first coffee. [Service Metrics](/service-metrics/) shows the service's **total reach** — in-person and online attendance, together and separately — and the stream carries a **health score from 0 to 100** with plain-English notes. No text messages, no putting the tech volunteer on the spot, no wondering whether "it went fine" means *fine*.

Here's what that Monday look actually contains, and where each number comes from.

## "Did it work?" — the stream health score

Every streamed service gets a single 0-100 score summarizing what the encoder and booth computer experienced while live: bitrate stability, dropped frames, frame rate, encoder load, and the machine's own vital signs, sampled every five seconds all service long. A 94 means the online congregation had a smooth morning. A 61 comes with specific, readable reasons — and every deduction ships with a recommendation, so the score reads less like a verdict and more like a to-do list for the team.

The pastor doesn't need to parse any of the telemetry; that's the point of the number. But it changes the Monday conversation from "how was the stream?" (answer: a shrug) to "I saw we scored 88 with one reconnect — anything the team needs?" — which is a genuinely encouraging question to receive in the booth. The full scoring system is explained in [reading your stream health score](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/).

Two companion signals round out the "did it work" picture:

- **The alert feed.** [Pulse monitoring](/av-monitoring/) watches every booth machine and device around the clock and keeps 90 days of alert history. A quiet feed is trustworthy by design — thresholds and anti-noise rules are [published exactly](/kb/articles/pulse-alert-thresholds-reference/) — so silence really does mean healthy.
- **The self-healing log.** If the stream restarted itself at 10:42 and nobody in the room noticed, [the log shows it](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/). Leadership seeing that the system quietly handled a problem is very different from leadership never knowing there was one.

## "Are we growing?" — total reach, honestly counted

The room is only half the congregation. The homebound member who gave faithfully for fifty years, the family three states away, the not-yet-visitor who's been quietly watching for a month deciding whether your church is safe to walk into — they all worshiped with you, and a headcount on a sticky note misses every one of them.

Service Metrics counts both halves:

- **In the room:** volunteers use a seat-map counter in Tech Manager — tap a seat to toggle it, hold to mark a member or first-time visitor — or camera Auto-Count tallies the room automatically from an RTSP camera or webcam, on a schedule or on demand. (Setup for that is in the [camera Auto-Count guide](/kb/articles/camera-auto-count-attendance-setup/).)
- **Online:** connect your YouTube or Facebook broadcast and viewers are counted automatically — concurrent viewers sampled every minute, with peak and average rolled into that day's attendance when the broadcast ends. Streams through the WorshipMetrics player count the same way.

**Total reach** is the sum a leader can actually use: everyone who worshiped, with the in-person and online shapes always visible separately. And because every service is stored the same way, Attendance Over Time answers the questions leadership meetings actually ask — Easter versus last Easter, this sermon series versus the last one, whether the online congregation is growing or quietly going stagnant — by month, quarter, or year, per service type.

## What this changes for the tech team

It's worth saying plainly: the goal isn't surveillance of the booth, and the gracious framing matters. What the dashboard removes is the *interrogation* — the volunteer who gave up their Sunday morning no longer fields "so did it work?" texts, because the answer is self-serve. What it adds is recognition: a string of healthy scores is visible evidence of work that used to be invisible precisely when it was done well.

It also makes the ask for resources concrete. "We need a better internet plan" lands differently when the health reports show bitrate sagging during greeting time three weeks running. Numbers turn hunches into a budget line — our guide to [AV budget planning](/kb/articles/av-budget-planning-churches/) picks up that thread.
