## The 8:45 test

Every support arrangement a church has ever made gets graded by exactly one exam, and it's never scheduled: **the pre-service failure.** It's 8:45. The stream won't start, or [OBS won't launch](/kb/articles/obs-wont-launch-sunday-morning/), or the encoder is [throwing warnings](/kb/articles/obs-encoding-overloaded-during-worship/) — and the congregation arrives in seventy-five minutes.

Now run your current support plan against that clock. The forums that helped so much on Tuesday are asleep. The integrator's voicemail says Monday–Friday. The manufacturer's line opens at 9 a.m. Pacific. And the volunteer who always fixes it is at their niece's wedding, phone silenced, which is [the single point of failure the whole plan was quietly resting on](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/). This is the gap that makes church tech support different from every other kind: **your failures have a service time, and it's the one time nobody answers.**

Here's what a plan that passes the 8:45 test looks like — in the order the layers actually engage.

## First responder: the automation that already answered

The best 8:45 call is the one that never happens. Before a human dials anything, [self-healing](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) has been on duty all morning: a stream down more than 30 seconds restarts itself, a stopped recording restarts on detection, a network blip gets one clean queued reconnect, and a filling disk cleans its own cache. The alert on your phone frequently arrives already reading *resolved* — so the honest first move at 8:45 is to [check the dashboard](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/) before assuming crisis. A meaningful share of Sunday emergencies are twenty seconds of buffering wearing a scary costume.

## Second responder: the line that answers at any hour

For what automation can't fix, [910-WORSHIP](/phone-support/) answers — and at 8:45 on a Sunday, that means **Journey**, the AI that picks up the phone around the clock. The word *AI* undersells what makes it useful: Journey knows your *actual system* — the same live inventory and [Signal Map](/system-documentation/) that [Luminos](/luminos-ai-assistant/) reads in the app, down to cable and port labels — so "the confidence monitor is dark" gets an answer about your confidence monitor, its cable, and its port. On the free tier you get smart help inside alerts and one full session to try; Assist ($49/mo) removes every cap: unlimited, 24/7, no queue, no "have you tried turning it off."

For a volunteer alone in the booth — [increasingly a student](/kb/articles/youth-group-already-knows-obs/), which is exactly as it should be — a line that always answers and already knows the system is the difference between a scary morning and a guided one.

## Third responder: the human whose Sunday is your Sunday

Some mornings need a person — judgment, experience, a calm voice that's seen this before. That's the **Sunday Assurance** add-on, and it's built for precisely this exam: a production professional on call *every single Sunday*, who can see your booth — live telemetry, stream state, [the settings this stream is running](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/), what self-healing already tried — the moment the call connects. No describing your setup. No archaeology. The call starts at *"I can see it — here's what we're doing."*

And it's worth knowing who's behind the number, because it explains the design: there's no call center. WorshipMetrics is a pastor, an engineer, and an AV educator who've spent their careers in church production — Paul still answers 910-WORSHIP himself. The support was built by people who've *had* the 8:45 morning, which is why every layer of it assumes the clock is running.

## The layer under all of it: your own bench

One more honest note, because [this whole series keeps arriving at it](/kb/articles/church-av-tech-support-options/): the churches that sail through 8:45 mornings usually didn't need the call at all — because a [second trained person](/kb/articles/train-obs-volunteers-church/) was in the booth, or a [mentor was watching from home](/kb/articles/obs-help-from-home-sunday/) and fixed it in a text. External support is the floor, not the plan. The plan is [a team](/kb/articles/get-obs-volunteers-church/), with a floor under it that never sleeps.

Run the 8:45 test on your church this week — on a calm Tuesday, ask "who would we actually call?" out loud. If the answer is one name and a prayer, you now know exactly which layers to add, and every one of them can be live before Sunday.
