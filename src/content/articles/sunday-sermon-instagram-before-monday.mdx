## The short answer: record through the platform, pick from a ranked list, post the vertical render

If your service is recorded in WorshipMetrics, the path from "pastor said something great" to "it's on Instagram" doesn't involve editing software or an hour of scrubbing. The pipeline watches the whole service so you don't have to: it transcribes the recording, has AI score and rank the shareable moments, and renders your picks as finished vertical clips — captions burned in, your branding applied. A volunteer with a phone and twenty minutes on Sunday afternoon can genuinely have a clip live before dinner.

Here's the realistic timeline, stage by stage.

## 12:10 p.m. — the recording is already in the library

The step most teams overlook is the one that requires nothing: if you stream through [WorshipMetrics live streaming](/live-streaming/), every service records automatically and the finished recording lands in your library without anyone pressing a button. There is no "wait, did we record?" moment, because recording isn't a task — it's a property of streaming. (Tech Manager even [restarts a stopped recording automatically](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/), which is the failure you'd otherwise discover on Monday, in regret.)

Even better: if your operator flagged a moment live — "grab the last 30 seconds of that" — those booth clips register as Reels source videos automatically. The moment that made the room go quiet at 10:40 is already waiting in the pipeline at noon.

## 12:30 p.m. — AI hands you a ranked list, not a blank timeline

This is where the hour of scrubbing disappears. The pipeline transcribes the service with church vocabulary boosted — books of the Bible, phrases like "altar call," your pastor's and church's names — because a caption that mangles "Habakkuk" isn't postable, and captions get burned into the final video.

Then Claude, Anthropic's AI model, scores the candidate moments. Each one arrives with three things: a **category** (one of ten — sermon point, prayer, altar call, welcome, and so on, which keeps announcement clips out of your feed), a **shareability score from 0 to 100** estimating how well the moment stands alone to someone who has never visited your church, and an **eight-word hook line** you can use or rewrite. Quality badges — Quotable, Emotional, Teaching, Call to Action, Uplifting — give you a fast visual filter between strong candidates.

Your Sunday-afternoon job is reviewing the top five of a ranked list, not hunting through 75 minutes of timeline. The full machinery is described in [how AI finds your sermon moments](/kb/articles/how-ai-finds-sermon-moments/); the editorial craft — which moments travel well, pacing, framing — is in the [sermon clipping guide](/kb/articles/clipping-sermons-social-media/).

## 12:50 p.m. — render once, get every format

A chosen moment renders into the three aspect ratios that cover essentially every platform:

| Format | Resolution | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 | Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Stories |
| Square 1:1 | 1080 × 1080 | Feed posts |
| Widescreen 16:9 | 1920 × 1080 | YouTube, website embeds, sermon pages |

Captions are burned in by default — the overwhelming majority of social video plays muted, so captions are the reason anyone stops scrolling — with four style presets and adjustable position, scale, and sync. Branding is applied at render time: lower-third styles, font presets, your color and logo, optional intro and outro. Every clip leaves the pipeline looking like it came from your church, not from a template.

## 1:15 p.m. — posting

One honest distinction here. Finished renders can **publish directly to YouTube** — each format is its own publish item, so the vertical Short and the widescreen clip go out side by side without exporting twice. Instagram and TikTok don't accept third-party scheduled uploads the same way, so for those you **download the 9:16 render and post it from the app** — thirty seconds on the phone that's already in your pocket. The pipeline's job was getting you a finished, branded, captioned file; the last tap is yours.

That's the whole Sunday: recording appeared on its own, AI ranked the moments, you picked one, and the render was Instagram-ready before the afternoon nap.

## The privacy question, answered before it's asked

Some churches aren't comfortable uploading full service video to any cloud — pastoral prayer, baptism testimonies, and care moments end up on recordings. For those teams there's a fully supported **local mode**: [Tech Manager](/tech-manager/) transcribes on the booth computer using Whisper running entirely locally, analyzes and renders locally, and sends **only short clip text** to the cloud for scoring — never audio, never video. Same scoring brain, same three formats, different privacy posture. Neither mode is a second-class citizen.
