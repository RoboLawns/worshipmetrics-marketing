## How to use this guide when the service starts in twenty minutes

This is a field guide, not a lecture. Scan the ten symptoms below, find the one on your screen, and run its fast checks **in order** — each one links to a full deep dive if the quick version doesn't land the fix. Two rules make every fix below safer: **change one thing at a time**, and **say out loud (or type in the team chat) what you touched**. Half of next week's mystery is this week's undocumented fix.

And if you already know your real problem is that you're back on this page *every* week — skip to [the two upgrades](#when-the-list-stops-helping-two-upgrades-that-change-the-game) at the end. A booth that rotates through a [different problem every Sunday](/kb/articles/obs-problem-every-sunday/) doesn't have ten problems; it has one. (This guide is part of a series — the booth's other front doors are [vMix troubleshooting](/kb/articles/vmix-troubleshooting-guide-church/) and [ProPresenter troubleshooting](/kb/articles/propresenter-troubleshooting-guide-church/).) And the best troubleshooting is the kind you prevent — the pre-service half of this page exists as a [printable one-page checklist](/sunday-checklist/) you can tape to the booth desk.

## 1. A source went black — camera, display capture, or NDI

Check the obvious two first: is the right scene live, and is the source's eye icon actually on? Then follow the signal *backward*: does the capture device's own utility see a picture? If yes, the problem is inside OBS (re-select the device in source properties); if no, it's the cable or the camera, and OBS was never the culprit. Laptop display capture showing black is its own famous gotcha — the capture is running on the wrong GPU.

Deep dives: [OBS black screen fixes](/kb/troubleshooting/obs-black-screen-church-stream/), [NDI camera not showing up](/kb/troubleshooting/ndi-camera-not-showing-obs-vmix/), and [HDMI signal dropping or going black](/kb/troubleshooting/hdmi-signal-dropping-church-setup/).

## 2. "Failed to connect to server" — the stream won't start

Nine times out of ten this is the stream key or the destination, not your network: keys rotate when someone clicks "End Stream" on the platform side, and a copied-with-a-trailing-space key fails silently. Re-paste the key, confirm the right service and server, and try once more before touching anything else. If the platform itself is down, no local fix exists — check its status page before you rebuild your settings in a panic.

Deep dives: [OBS not connecting to YouTube or Twitch](/kb/troubleshooting/obs-not-connecting-twitch-youtube/), [stream key invalid errors](/kb/troubleshooting/stream-key-invalid-church-fix/), and [Facebook Live connection failures](/kb/troubleshooting/facebook-live-connection-failed-church/).

## 3. Dropped frames — the network can't carry your bitrate

Dropped frames (the indicator going orange or red) are a *network* symptom, distinct from encoding trouble. Fast sequence: confirm you're on wired ethernet and not WiFi, kill anything else eating upload (cloud backup on the office PC counts), and if it persists, drop your bitrate 25% live — [a conservative stream that holds beats an ambitious one that collapses](/kb/articles/obs-studio-complete-church-guide/).

Deep dives: [dropping frames during a church stream](/kb/troubleshooting/stream-dropping-frames-church/), [is our church WiFi good enough?](/kb/articles/church-wifi-good-enough-to-livestream/), and [how much bandwidth streaming really needs](/kb/troubleshooting/church-network-bandwidth-streaming/).

## 4. "Encoding overloaded" — the computer can't keep up

The opposite problem: your machine, not your network. Fast sequence: close everything that isn't OBS, drop the encoder preset one notch (quality falls less than you fear), and if the machine has a capable GPU, move from x264 to the hardware encoder. This warning loves to appear *mid-worship*, when six cameras, motion backgrounds, and a browser source all peak together.

Deep dive: [why OBS says "Encoding Overloaded" in the middle of worship](/kb/articles/obs-encoding-overloaded-during-worship/), plus [bitrate and resolution settings that fit your machine](/kb/software/obs/obs-bitrate-settings-church/).

## 5. The stream is silent

Look at the OBS audio mixer before anything else: are the meters moving? Meters moving but no audio online means a muted track or monitoring misdirection; meters dead means the mixer feed stopped upstream — most often a USB device that re-enumerated and OBS is now listening to the wrong "USB Audio Device." Recordings silent but stream fine (or vice versa) is track assignment, not hardware.

Deep dives: [no audio on YouTube or Facebook](/kb/troubleshooting/no-audio-youtube-facebook-stream/), [mixer USB not recognized](/kb/troubleshooting/mixer-usb-not-recognized-computer/), and [audio routing from your church mixer](/kb/software/obs/obs-audio-routing-church-mixer/).

## 6. Audio and video drift apart

If they start together and separate over the service, that's drift — usually a sample-rate mismatch (44.1 kHz device in a 48 kHz world) or a capture device buffering under load. If they're offset by the same amount all service, that's latency — fix it with a sync offset on the source, measured once, written down.

Deep dives: [audio and video out of sync](/kb/troubleshooting/audio-delay-video-stream-fix/), [why OBS audio drifts by the end of the sermon](/kb/articles/obs-audio-drift-long-services/), and [camera video lagging behind audio](/kb/troubleshooting/camera-lagging-behind-audio-stream/).

## 7. The preview looks perfect, the stream looks bad

The preview is your machine rendering at full quality; the stream is that picture squeezed through your bitrate and the platform's transcode. Blurry-only-online means the encoder settings, not the cameras: bitrate too low for the resolution, or a downscale mismatch. The fix lives in three settings, not in new gear.

Deep dive: [why OBS looks perfect in preview but blurry on the stream](/kb/articles/obs-preview-fine-stream-looks-bad/).

## 8. Fine at 9:00, struggling by 9:40

The signature of *accumulation*: heat, memory creep, a filling disk, a browser source leaking — nothing is wrong at the start, everything is marginal forty minutes in. This is the failure that convinces teams they're haunted, because every pre-service test passes. The tell is in the curve, not the snapshot — which is why unwatched booths never catch it.

Deep dives: [why the livestream stutters forty minutes in](/kb/articles/livestream-stutters-40-minutes-in/), and [what happens if the booth PC crashes mid-service](/kb/articles/booth-pc-crashes-mid-service/).

## 9. OBS won't launch, crashed, or the recording won't play

For launch failures: safe mode first (it disables plugins and third-party sources — if OBS opens clean, [a plugin is your suspect](/kb/articles/obs-plugins-church-stream-safety/)). For a crash that took the recording with it: stop writing to that drive and try the remux — and switch future recordings to MKV so a crash can never hold a service hostage again.

Deep dives: [OBS won't launch and the service starts in an hour](/kb/articles/obs-wont-launch-sunday-morning/), and [saving an unplayable recording after a crash](/kb/articles/obs-recording-unplayable-after-crash/).

## 10. It worked last Sunday — someone changed something

The most human failure on the list. A guest operator "improved" a scene, an update reset an encoder, someone clicked through a dialog at rehearsal. Without a baseline you're archaeology-ing your own booth at 8:40 a.m. With one — exported profiles and scene collections, settings captured per service — "what changed?" is a thirty-second diff instead of an interrogation.

Deep dives: [ending the between-Sundays settings mystery](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/), and [letting more than one person run OBS safely](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-obs-church/).
