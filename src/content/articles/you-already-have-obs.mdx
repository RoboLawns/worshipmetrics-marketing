## The best free decision your church ever made — half-finished

Somewhere in your church's past, someone chose OBS. Maybe deliberately, maybe because it was free and the tutorial was good. Either way, the choice was excellent: OBS Studio is professional-grade, endlessly capable, costs nothing, and carries a staggering share of the world's church streams every Sunday.

And then — like almost every church — you parked **one volunteer** in front of it and called the booth done. That person learned everything, runs everything, and now *is* everything: operator, monitor, troubleshooter, trainer-who-never-gets-to-train. The software scaled beautifully. The structure never did — and every recurring Sunday problem you have, from [rotating mystery failures](/kb/articles/obs-problem-every-sunday/) to [the settings whodunit](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/) to the [burnout math](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/), traces back to that half-finished decision.

Here's the finished version: the same OBS machine, wrapped in an ecosystem, carrying a team.

## What one OBS system becomes with structure around it

[WorshipMetrics](/obs-integration/) connects to OBS two ways, and the pricing logic matters for a volunteer budget: **local control in the booth is free, on every plan, forever.** The [Tech Manager](/tech-manager/) desktop app talks to OBS on the local network, watches the machine and the encoder [every five seconds](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/), applies the [self-healing rules](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) — dropped stream restarted, stopped recording recovered — and documents the booth. The paid layer (Assist, $49/mo; Stream, $99/mo per campus) takes it beyond the building: authorized helpers see the booth's live state from anywhere and can **switch scenes and start or stop the stream and recording** over OBS's own WebSocket protocol.

That combination — visibility, automation, remote reach — is what turns one chair into many, because the thing that kept everything on one person was never the workload. It was that *only they could see, fix, and be trusted with the system.* Once the system is visible and safe, it divides.

## The seats, drawn around your actual booth

- **The OBS operator** — the seat you have, finally allowed to be *one* job.
- **The remote mentor** — the veteran who [watches and assists from anywhere](/kb/articles/obs-help-from-home-sunday/): scenes, stream, and recording state live, no more troubleshooting-by-phone-description.
- **Cameras, lyrics, audio** — the classic seats, each a [real role with a real page](/kb/articles/serving-opportunities-teens-without-stage/), each teachable in an hour.
- **The monitoring seat** — a student watching [Pulse](/av-monitoring/) and the meters; the person who catches [encoder load climbing](/kb/articles/obs-encoding-overloaded-during-worship/) at 85% instead of at "overloaded."
- **The archivist and the clips editor** — [recordings that never get lost](/kb/articles/obs-recording-unplayable-after-crash/), and [Sunday's sermon on Instagram by Monday](/kb/articles/sunday-sermon-instagram-before-monday/), from a couch.
- **Documentation and scheduling** — the [Signal Map](/system-documentation/) keeper and the [matrix](/kb/articles/volunteer-scheduling-without-text-scramble/) coordinator: the quiet seats that make everything else survivable.

Nine-ish seats, one free encoder. And because OBS costs nothing and its local control costs nothing, **this is the lowest-barrier team-building platform in church tech** — the whole stack can start before a dollar is spent.

## The pipeline that fills the seats

Where do nine people come from? [The youth room, mostly](/kb/articles/need-volunteers-church-livestream/) — the students already streaming, editing, and living in the exact instincts these seats run on. The [seat finder](/tools/church-av-seat-finder/) matches them, [the training machinery](/kb/articles/train-vmix-operators-without-risking-sunday/) makes their first Sundays [safe by design](/kb/articles/students-run-church-livestream-safely/), and the [AV Club structure](/kb/articles/start-church-av-club-vmix/) makes the whole thing self-renewing — older students training younger ones, a ladder from tablet to the operator's chair, and [a booth that survives anyone leaving](/kb/articles/when-your-vmix-person-leaves/).

## Finish the decision

You already made the hard choice well — the encoder is excellent and paid for. What's left is the half nobody told you about: install Tech Manager beside OBS this week (free), read one [health report](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/) next Monday, then hand *one seat* to *one named person* with a mentor beside them. That's the whole first month.

The software was never the ceiling. The chair count was. Add chairs.
