## The short answer: yes — locally for free, remotely on the paid plans

The question usually arrives from one of two Sundays: the veteran is sick and someone asks *"can anyone else even run this?"* — or a new volunteer is at the keyboard and the veteran wishes they could see what's happening without driving in. Both have the same answer, and it's built into OBS itself.

OBS speaks a remote-control protocol called **WebSocket (v5)** — a front door the OBS project maintains for exactly this. [WorshipMetrics](/obs-integration/) uses it two ways:

- **In the booth, free forever:** [Tech Manager](/tech-manager/) connects to OBS on the local network — control, monitoring, and the [self-healing rules](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) — at no cost, on every plan.
- **From anywhere, on Assist ($49/mo) or Stream ($99/mo per campus):** authorized people on your team see the booth's live state from any device and can **switch scenes and start or stop the stream and the recording.** One secure outbound connection from the booth; no port forwarding, no VPN, no remote-desktop wrestling. Every remote action lands in the support history with a name attached.

## What that makes possible on an actual Sunday

**The confirmation nobody has to drive in for.** "Is the recording actually rolling?" — answered from a phone, and *fixed* from a phone if it isn't. The classic pre-service panic becomes a ten-second check by whoever's on call.

**A second operator, anywhere.** The scheduled volunteer is stuck in traffic; the backup starts the stream from their kitchen and switches to the countdown scene. The service opens on time, and the [first-Sunday rookie](/kb/articles/diary-first-sunday-stream-operator/) at the desk never faced the moment alone.

**Mentoring with eyes, not guesses.** This is the quiet revolution for [training](/kb/articles/train-vmix-operators-without-risking-sunday/): a veteran watches the *actual state* — current scene, stream status, [encoder health streaming in every five seconds](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/) — while the trainee drives. Coaching becomes "you're still on the wide shot" instead of "describe your screen to me," which is the whole difference between [help from home that works](/kb/articles/obs-help-from-home-sunday/) and a twenty-minute phone archaeology.

## The honest boundary — and why it's the right one

Remote OBS control covers the **live-operation surface**: scenes, stream, recording, with state visible. It does *not* reach OBS's deep editing — building scenes, adding sources, audio filter surgery. That work stays at the booth machine, on a weekday, in [an owned profile with a settings baseline](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/) — which is honestly where it belongs. Sunday-morning remote settings surgery is how streams die; the boundary enforces the discipline every stable booth adopts anyway: **operate live, edit on Tuesdays.**

(For churches running vMix alongside or instead, the remote surface goes deeper — [the full switching panel](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/) — but the OBS story above is complete on its own.)

## What "more than one person" is really about

Notice what every scenario above quietly assumes: *there's a second person to hand things to.* That's the real unlock, and it's why this question is bigger than its plumbing. The reason one volunteer became irreplaceable was never that OBS is hard — it's that nobody else could **see** the system, **fix** it safely, or **be trusted** with it. WebSocket control plus [telemetry](/av-monitoring/) plus [logged actions](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) dissolves all three barriers at once — which is why churches that turn this on don't just get redundancy. They get [a team](/kb/articles/you-already-have-obs/): seats, a ladder, [students from the youth room](/kb/articles/need-volunteers-church-livestream/), and a veteran who finally takes a vacation with their phone in their pocket instead of their car keys.

So: yes, more than one person can run your OBS setup. The better news is what that makes possible — and if your booth has been [fighting a different fire every week](/kb/articles/obs-problem-every-sunday/) with one exhausted firefighter, this is the door out.
