## Your vMix, everywhere it needs to be — still living in your booth

vMix is a superb church switcher, and if your church runs it, you've already got the hard part. What you probably *don't* have is a way for anyone but the person in the chair to see or touch it. Cloud vMix production closes that gap — and the WorshipMetrics version does it without moving your production an inch off the machine you own.

Here's the architecture in one sentence: **vMix keeps running locally in your booth, and [WorshipMetrics](/cloud-control/) mirrors its live state and relays its control APIs to any device your team signs in from.** The switch happens where it always did — free, instant, reliable. The cloud is just the glass that lets your people reach through to it.

## What "the full panel" actually includes

This is the part that distinguishes vMix in the ecosystem: because vMix exposes real control over its **HTTP and TCP APIs**, the cloud panel isn't a viewer — it's the switching surface. From a laptop at home or a phone in the overflow room, an authorized operator gets:

- **Cut, and every transition** — fade, wipe, zoom, stinger — with adjustable duration, and a hands-on **T-bar**.
- **Preview and program** switching across every input, with **live thumbnails** of each input beside an enlargeable program view — so anyone with access sees exactly what's on screen and what's queued.
- **Overlays 1–4**, input play/pause, and **bus audio** — mute and volume, the everyday moves of a service, one click each.
- **Master and bus meters** streamed live beside the video — the same numbers vMix shows, in the panel.

Start and stop the stream and the recording from the panel too — and let [a second person confirm the red light is actually on](/kb/articles/multiple-operators-vmix-church/) before the prelude ends. (Running an ATEM alongside vMix? Its cut/preview/program control stays local in the booth, with HyperDeck status monitored alongside.)

## Why local-switch-plus-cloud beats renting vMix in the cloud

You *can* rent a vMix instance on a cloud server. For most churches you shouldn't, and the reasons are the same three that make [local-first the right church architecture](/kb/articles/cloud-video-production-for-churches/) generally:

- **Your live switch never depends on the internet.** Cloud-hosted vMix means every cut travels a network round-trip; a bad-bandwidth Sunday degrades your *switching*, not just your stream. Local vMix cuts instantly regardless, and [self-healing steps the stream down](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) if the upload sags — the service in the room never stutters.
- **No compute bill.** You already own a vMix machine. Local control is [free forever](/pricing/); you add the cloud panel, not a server rental.
- **You keep doing it yourselves.** This isn't outsourcing your production — it's giving your own volunteers a bigger, reachable cockpit.

## What it unlocks on a Sunday

The panel is the feature; the *team* is the point. With the full surface reachable from anywhere:

- The tech director on vacation [watches preview and program from their phone](/kb/articles/volunteers-run-livestream-without-tech-director/) and talks a new volunteer through a tricky transition — or takes the switch for ninety seconds and hands it back.
- A [student learns on the real board](/kb/articles/train-vmix-operators-without-risking-sunday/) while a mentor watches the identical live state remotely, catching mistakes in preview before they hit program.
- A second campus, a sick operator, an ambitious Christmas service — all become staffable without another expert physically in the room.

Every remote action runs through Tech Manager and lands in your support history with a name attached; only people you've invited can connect; and it's all one **secure outbound connection** — no port forwarding, no VPN, no firewall holes. The whole story of turning one vMix system into a team is in [you already have vMix](/kb/articles/you-already-have-vmix/).
