## The short answer: OBS's price tag is also its support model

Here's the trade nobody mentions when a church adopts OBS: the software is free because it's open-source — built by a community, supported by a community. There is no 1-800-OBS. When something breaks, the official path is the [OBS forums and documentation](https://obsproject.com/forum/): genuinely excellent, deeply knowledgeable, and operating on *forum time*. You post logs, kind strangers respond, the thread resolves in days.

For a hobbyist streamer, that model is fine. For a church, the math fails on one specific fact: **your failures have a service time.** The [encoder overload](/kb/articles/obs-encoding-overloaded-during-worship/) happens during the second song. The [launch failure](/kb/articles/obs-wont-launch-sunday-morning/) happens at 9:02. The volunteer at the keyboard — increasingly [a student](/kb/articles/youth-group-already-knows-obs/), which is good — needs an answer in ninety seconds, not a thread by Tuesday. So "who do you call when OBS breaks?" is the right question, and the honest answer is a *stack*, not a number. Four layers, cheapest first.

## Layer 1: the support requests that never get made

The best support ticket is the one automation closed before anyone noticed. A meaningful share of church OBS emergencies are routine failures with known fixes — and [self-healing](/kb/articles/tech-manager-self-healing-explained/) applies them in seconds: a dropped stream restarts after 30 seconds down, a [stopped recording](/kb/articles/obs-recording-unplayable-after-crash/) restarts on detection, a filling disk cleans its cache, a network blip queues one clean reconnect. Every action logged; the genuinely ambiguous cases — a real crash — escalate to [a human phone](/kb/articles/church-stream-drop-phone-alert/) instead of being papered over. Install this layer and your support volume drops before you've called anyone.

## Layer 2: helpers who can see instead of ask

The worst part of any support call is the archaeology — *"describe your screen to me."* Cloud-connected OBS deletes it: whoever is helping [sees the booth's actual state](/kb/articles/is-obs-stream-actually-working/) — stream, recording, scenes, machine vitals streaming in every few seconds — plus [the settings every stream ran with](/kb/articles/obs-settings-changed-between-sundays/) and the history of what automation already tried. Diagnosis starts at fact, not description. This is also what makes your *own* people effective as first-line support: the [mentor at home](/kb/articles/obs-help-from-home-sunday/) who can see the panel — and act on it — resolves most Sunday moments in a text.

## Layer 3: a team, so "support" isn't one overloaded person

Be honest about who currently answers your church's OBS support calls: the same exhausted producer who runs everything — which means your support plan and your [burnout problem](/kb/articles/church-tech-burnout-cycle/) are the same fact. The durable fix is depth: [more trained hands](/kb/articles/train-obs-volunteers-church/), each owning [a seat](/kb/articles/you-already-have-obs/), with a monitoring seat that catches trouble early and [a recruiting pipeline](/kb/articles/get-obs-volunteers-church/) that keeps the bench full. A booth with a team doesn't just produce fewer emergencies — it has more people qualified to answer the ones that happen.

## Layer 4: the number that answers — and already knows you

Some Sundays exceed everyone in the building, and this is the layer churches running free software assumed they could never have: **professional support with context.** A call to [910-WORSHIP](/phone-support/) starts from your system's record — the live telemetry, the [health reports](/kb/articles/reading-your-stream-health-score/), the [Signal Map of your actual wiring](/system-documentation/), the self-healing log — so the conversation opens at *"I can see the bitrate sagged at 9:38; let's look at your network"* instead of twenty questions. And with the **Sunday Assurance** add-on, a production pro is on call *every single Sunday*, your booth on their screen the moment the call connects.

## The stack, assembled

Keep the OBS forums in your bookmarks — for Tuesday deep-dives, they remain the best free resource in streaming. But let Sunday run on the stack: automation closing tickets before they open, visibility replacing description, [a team](/kb/articles/obs-problem-every-sunday/) replacing the lone hero, and a number that answers with your booth already on screen. That's what "tech support for free software" looks like when a church builds it properly — and every layer doubles as the safety net that lets you [hand the next generation the keys](/kb/articles/students-run-church-livestream-safely/).
